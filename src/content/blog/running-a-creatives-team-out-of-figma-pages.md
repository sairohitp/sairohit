As Creatives and Photography Team Lead at Google Developer Group, SRM Institute of Science and Technology, I led a team of more than 25 people who designed everything that carried the club's name: event identities, posts, stories, posters, reels and certificates. We did not use a project management tool for design. We used Figma pages.

## A page for every member

For campaigns with several designers, each member got their own page in the shared file. The Freshers Highlights file has pages named after the people who worked on it, and so does the 6k followers story. Everyone could see each other's directions, borrow ideas, and nobody overwrote anyone else's work.

When a direction won, it moved to the Main or Final page. Everything else stayed, credited by page name.

## An Editor page for reviews

Nineteen of the campaign files have an Editor page. That is where reviews happened: notes placed next to the frames, in the language we actually spoke. One reads "keep it clean first post main thik hain" (the first post is fine, keep it clean). Another reminds the designer not to copy components everywhere, because the library had already been published for the project.

Reviews on the canvas are faster than meetings and leave a record that the next designer can learn from.

## Templates and a shared library

Recurring formats had templates: event stories, speaker posts, results posts and certificates. A published component library meant logos, colour, type and the dotted matrix pattern stayed consistent across dozens of posts made by different people.

## Measuring what mattered

The work showed up in the club's reach. Over my time leading the team, our social and brand reach grew by about 60%, and the team kept a steady rhythm of event campaigns, festival posts and recruitment drives.

## What I would pass on

- **Give everyone a page.** Ownership and credit come for free.
- **Review on the canvas.** Notes next to the work beat feedback in a chat thread.
- **Publish a library early.** Consistency should not depend on who is designing that day.
- **Keep the drafts.** Today's rejected direction is next month's starting point.
