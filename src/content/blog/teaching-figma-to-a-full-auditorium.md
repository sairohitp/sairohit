As the Creatives and Photography Team Lead at Google Developer Group, SRM Institute of Science and Technology, I gave a keynote on UI/UX design fundamentals to 1,200+ students and faculty. Here is how I structured it, and what I would keep doing.

## Start with the tool, not the theory

I started with the basics: explaining the interface and how to move objects around. In a room that size, many people are opening Figma for the first time. If the first ten minutes feel confusing, you lose them for the rest of the session.

## Make the first things fun

Before any wireframes, we drew fun illustrations together: the Figma logo, a chemical flask and a teddy bear. Simple shapes, quick wins, and something to show the person sitting next to you. It builds confidence and teaches the core tools without calling it a lesson.

## Build up to something real

From illustrations we moved to an interactive level, and then to the wireframe and high-fidelity design of a personal blog. Each step reused what the last one taught, so the final design felt like a natural next step rather than a leap.

## Watch the room, not the slides

I was particularly astonished when the ground force team reported that more than 90% of the audience actively followed the instructions. That created a positive, engaging momentum throughout the auditorium, even reaching the last row.

That number came from the team walking the aisles, not from the stage. In a large room, the people moving between rows are your best feedback loop: they see who is stuck long before the speaker does.

## What I would keep

- **Teach by making.** Everyone should have something on their canvas within minutes.
- **Sequence for confidence.** Fun, then useful, then real.
- **Use your team as sensors.** Pace the session on what they see.
- **Share the file.** The session’s Figma file is linked on my About page, so anyone can keep going on their own.

Teaching design to a room full of first-timers is a design problem in itself: an onboarding flow, delivered live.
