At Google Developer Student Clubs, SRM IST, workshops ended with a certificate. Certificates are made to be shared, often straight to LinkedIn, which makes them one of the most visible designs of the whole event. They are worth designing properly.

## One layout, many names

A certificate is a template that will be filled hundreds of times, so the design starts with the variable parts:

- **The recipient's name,** which can be short or very long.
- **The role,** such as participant or member of the organising committee.
- **The event and date.** The Design and Develop certificates, for example, recognise members of the organising committee of the workshop held on 11 March 2024.
- **A certificate ID and date of issue,** so it can be verified.

The layout has to hold a long name on one line without shrinking it into nothing, so the name sits in a generous box with room to grow.

## Signatures carry the credibility

Each certificate is signed by the club's Team Lead and the Faculty Advisor, with their names and titles typed beneath. For Google Cloud Study Jam, the Program Facilitator signed as well. Those names do more for trust than any decoration.

## On brand, not loud

The Code with Node and Cloud Study Jam certificates use Google Sans and the club's colours, so they look like part of the same event as the posters and stories. The Design and Develop certificates borrow the workshop's own display type, Phonk, paired with ABeeZee. Either way, the design leaves plenty of white space. People screenshot and crop certificates; busy edges get cut off.

## The certificate as a product feature

In the events portal I designed, the certificate became part of the product. After an event, attendees see their certificate ("Presented to" with their name), a prompt to share their accomplishment, and a download button, next to a short feedback question. The moment people feel proud is the best moment to ask how the event went.

## What I learned

- **Design for the longest name first.**
- **Make it verifiable** with an ID and an issue date.
- **Let real signatures do the work.**
- **Ask for feedback at the moment of pride.**
