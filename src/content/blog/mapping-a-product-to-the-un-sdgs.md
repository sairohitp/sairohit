The Google Solution Challenge asks student teams to build something that addresses the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. Our team, Greenwealth, was two people: Aman Agrawal and me, both pre-final-year computer science students at SRM. We had both seen how recyclables piled up around us with nowhere sensible to go, so we built an app that lets people sell household goods to nearby recycling centres or donate them to nearby NGOs.

## Start with the behaviour, not the goal

It is tempting to pick goals first and fit a product to them. We started with a behaviour we wanted to change: people keep recyclables because getting rid of them properly is a hassle. The product had to make doing the right thing easier than doing nothing.

That led to three core screens: choose your waste categories (paper, plastic, furniture, clothes), choose a pickup location on a map, and choose a pickup slot. Three decisions, then someone comes to you.

## Then map each feature to a goal

Only after the flow worked did we map it to the goals the app addresses: sustainable cities and communities (11), responsible consumption and production (12), climate action (13) and life on land (15). The test for each claim was simple: can we point to the screen that does it?

- **Responsible consumption:** selling or donating keeps goods in use.
- **Sustainable cities:** pickups route waste to proper recycling centres.
- **Climate action and life on land:** less waste in landfill and in the open.

## Motivation people can see

Doing good is easier to repeat when you can see it. The app rewards every pickup with eco-points, shows your position on a leaderboard, and shares community stories, such as volunteers completing a two-week clean-up of Marine Drive beach. A short "Recycling 101" guide helps first-timers know what is recyclable where they live.

## What made the pitch stronger

Our slides walked through the real app, screen by screen, instead of listing goals with icons. Anyone watching could follow the exact path from a cluttered cupboard to a pickup.

## Lessons

- **Design the behaviour change first.**
- **Every impact claim needs a screen behind it.**
- **Make progress visible** with points, ranks and stories.
- **Show the product, not the slogans.**
