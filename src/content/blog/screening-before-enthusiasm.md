PULSE is a blood donation app that Aman Agrawal and I designed at SRM. It connects donors, people who need blood, hospitals and blood banks. The most important design decision is also the least glamorous: before anyone gets excited about donating, the app checks whether they can.

## Why eligibility comes first

A willing donor who turns out to be ineligible has wasted a trip, a slot at the blood bank and some goodwill. Catching that on the phone, before booking, is kinder to everyone. So the home screen puts "Am I eligible?" right next to the actions people come for: find blood banks, open requests, campaigns, a guide and how much to give.

## Three questions, one at a time

The screening flow asks three questions, each on its own screen:

1. Do you have any medical conditions?
2. Do you have any tattoos?
3. Do you drink or smoke regularly?

If the answer is yes, a text box asks for details. One question per screen keeps the tone conversational and makes each answer feel considered rather than rushed through a form. Previous and Next buttons let people go back and change an answer without starting again.

## Ask sensitive questions gently

These are personal questions. The wording is plain and neutral, and the result screen explains itself. It says why the person appears eligible, based on what they shared, and reminds them that the blood bank will make the final check on the day. The app informs; it does not diagnose.

## Requests that feel urgent, calmly

When someone needs blood, the request flow asks for the blood group from a simple grid, the location on a map and the details the hospital needs, then confirms that the request has been placed and that the person will be notified when a donor accepts. A clear confirmation screen matters most in stressful moments: people need to know it worked.

## Lessons

- **Put the gatekeeping question first.** It saves everyone's time.
- **One sensitive question per screen,** in neutral language.
- **Explain results, and defer to professionals** for the final decision.
- **Confirm loudly when it matters.**
