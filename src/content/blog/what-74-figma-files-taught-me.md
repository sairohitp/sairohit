For this portfolio I did something slightly obsessive: I decoded all 74 of my Figma files, layer by layer, and put what was inside them on the site. Every case study now has an "Inside the files" section with its pages, frames, typefaces, colours and components.

The totals were humbling: 2,852 frames, 712,572 layers, 30,737 text layers, 1,700 prototype links and 205 notes left on the canvas.

## My pages tell a story

The most common page names across the files were Main, Editor, Page 1, Legacy, Final and Cover. Nineteen files have an Editor page, which is where review happened in the Creatives team: someone would leave notes, and the designer would fix the frame on the Main page. Twelve files have a Legacy page, where old versions went instead of being deleted.

I am glad I kept them. The Legacy page of the learning platform, for example, still holds 160 frames of earlier website designs, a full record of where the product started.

## 16% of my frames were unnamed

452 of 2,852 frames were still called something like "Frame 13606" or "Group 4". In a file only you open, that is harmless. In a handoff, it is a tax on everyone else, and it makes files impossible to search later. I now name frames as I make them, using the screen name and state, such as "Login (Error)" or "Courses (Drafts)", which is exactly how the best-organised files in my set were named.

## Notes are the real process record

The 205 notes are my favourite find. A Figma file usually shows the result; notes show the thinking. There is a type scale written on the canvas of the events portal: headings 20, body 14, small text 12 or 10. There is a Whimsical link to the user flow. And in one campaign file, a teammate's review in Hinglish: "thoda padding add kardo boxes main" (add a little padding inside the boxes).

That one line says more about how our team worked than any process diagram.

## Typefaces show the contexts

Inter is the most used typeface across the files, followed by Google Sans (club work for Google Developer Student Clubs) and SF Pro (iOS apps). Each project's typeface choice follows its platform, which is how it should be.

## What I would change

- **Name frames by screen and state,** from the first draft.
- **Keep a Cover and a Readme page** in every file, so anyone can open it cold.
- **Write decisions on the canvas.** Notes are cheap and they age well.
- **Keep Legacy pages.** Before-and-after is the best story a case study can tell.
